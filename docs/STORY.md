# RUNNERS FROM THE BLOCK

**A sci-fi extraction adventure. Story and world bible.**

> One neighborhood. One thousand survivors. One very bad scientist. An entire universe standing between them and home.

This document is canon. It contains spoilers for the entire campaign. To propose a change, open an issue titled `RFC: story`.

---

## 1. The beginning: The Day Everything Moved

There was nothing particularly special about the neighborhood of Westwood Pines.

It was a quiet suburban community covering roughly one square mile. There were houses, schools, a grocery store, a gas station, a church, a small shopping plaza, a fire station, a community park, and approximately 1,000 people who called it home.

People went to work. Kids rode bicycles. Neighbors argued about fences. Someone was always mowing their lawn too early on Saturday morning.

And at the very edge of the neighborhood, behind a large privacy fence and a collection of increasingly suspicious warning signs, lived a scientist named Dr. Richard "Rick" Halden.

Rick was brilliant.

Rick was also completely insane.

For eleven years, Rick had been working on something he called the Dimensional Displacement Engine. His theory was that parallel universes existed alongside our own, occupying nearly identical coordinates but separated by an invisible dimensional barrier.

He believed he could open a temporary window between these realities.

His original intention was simple: teleport a small object into another dimension and bring it back.

He tested it on a baseball.

The baseball disappeared.

Three days later, it returned covered in an unidentified metallic substance, with a temperature of negative 40 degrees, and a handwritten note attached to it.

The note read:

**STOP OPENING THE DOOR.**

Rick became obsessed.

He increased the engine's power, modified its containment system, and began experimenting with larger objects. His calculations suggested that he could eventually transport an entire building.

But he made one catastrophic mistake.

He calculated the displacement field using geographic coordinates instead of physical boundaries.

At 3:17 PM on a Tuesday, Rick activated the machine.

The entire neighborhood was inside its radius.

Every house. Every car. Every person. Every tree. Every mailbox. Every dog. Every shopping cart in the grocery store.

Nothing was destroyed.

Nothing was burned.

Nothing was even moved.

An entire square mile of suburban America was simply removed from Earth and placed somewhere else.

And then, just as suddenly, everything stopped.

The birds were gone.

The sky was wrong.

The sun was enormous and purple.

Beyond the neighborhood fences stretched an endless landscape of strange forests, abandoned megastructures, alien ruins, crashed machines, and things that had no business existing.

Rick's experiment had worked.

He had successfully teleported an entire neighborhood into a parallel universe.

He just had absolutely no idea how to bring it back.

---

## 2. The survivors: Westwood Pines

The first 72 hours were chaos.

People flooded the streets. Phones stopped connecting. Emergency services couldn't reach anyone. The local news station broadcast static. GPS showed that everyone was simultaneously nowhere.

The neighborhood's 1,000 residents quickly realized three things:

1. They were no longer on Earth.
2. They were not alone.
3. Whatever lived outside their neighborhood was not friendly.

The community eventually organized itself.

The high school became a command center. The grocery store became a rationing facility. The fire station became the medical center. Rick's laboratory was placed under armed guard.

A council of residents established the **Westwood Survival Authority**, responsible for maintaining order, allocating resources, and organizing expeditions into the surrounding world.

The neighborhood became known simply as **The Block**.

Its residents began calling the outside world **The Elsewhere**.

And the people who ventured outside to retrieve supplies, investigate ruins, and search for a way home became known as **Runners**.

That is where your story begins.

## 3. Choose your character

The player is one of the Runners. Before the first expedition, they choose one of three backgrounds.

Each character has a different relationship with the neighborhood, different abilities, and a different perspective on Rick's experiment.

### The Soldier

*Former military. Disciplined. Prepared for the worst.*

Before the displacement, the Soldier was a veteran living in Westwood Pines. When everything happened, they immediately began organizing neighborhood defenses.

Specialties:
- Weapon proficiency
- Heavy armor
- Combat and suppression
- Defensive equipment

Personal motivation: Protect the people who survived and make sure the neighborhood never falls.

### The Techy

*Curious. Resourceful. Dangerously intelligent.*

The Techy was a local electronics enthusiast, engineer, or computer specialist. They had previously helped Rick acquire equipment for his experiments without knowing what he was building.

Specialties:
- Hacking and robotics
- Device crafting
- Electronic surveillance
- Unlocking restricted facilities

Personal motivation: Understand the physics of this universe and reverse-engineer the machine that brought everyone here.

### The Rogue

*Unpredictable. Resourceful. Always looking for an angle.*

The Rogue was a local troublemaker, mechanic, opportunist, or underground trader. They knew every back alley and every shortcut in Westwood Pines.

Specialties:
- Stealth and infiltration
- Lockpicking
- Traps and distractions
- Loot detection and escape

Personal motivation: Survive, get rich, and find a way back to Earth before anyone else figures out how valuable this new world really is.

The three classes share the same overarching story, but certain missions, dialogue, shortcuts, and discoveries change depending on the character selected.

## 4. The world beyond the neighborhood

The Elsewhere is not simply another planet.

It is a parallel reality where civilizations have existed, collapsed, and been replaced countless times. Some structures appear thousands of years old. Others look as if they were built yesterday. Technology from completely different eras exists side by side.

The strangest part is that the world seems to have a memory.

Certain locations rearrange themselves. Machines sometimes recognize people they've never met. Radio transmissions occasionally contain voices from Earth.

And somewhere beyond the known territories is an enormous structure that appears to be connected to every dimensional anomaly in this universe.

The survivors don't know it yet, but Rick didn't teleport them into a random dimension.

Something in this universe noticed the arrival of Westwood Pines.

## 5. The main adventure campaign

The campaign is structured around expeditions. Each expedition has three increasingly dangerous areas, taking the player deeper into a specific mystery.

You don't simply visit three random locations. Each area reveals something that changes how you understand the next one.

There are five major expeditions.

### Expedition 01: The Dead City

*First major expedition. Supplies and exploration.*

**Story premise**

Three days after the displacement, food supplies are running dangerously low. The grocery store can only sustain the neighborhood for so long, and the generators are consuming more fuel than expected.

A radio operator intercepts a signal from an enormous city approximately 12 miles beyond the neighborhood.

The signal repeats:

> "Municipal Emergency Distribution Center. Civilian supplies available. Please proceed to designated collection point."

Rick believes the city might contain equipment capable of identifying their dimensional coordinates.

The first expedition is organized.

**Objective:** Recover food, medicine, batteries, and any technology that could help Rick understand their location.

#### Area 1: The Outskirts

The first expedition reaches the edge of an enormous abandoned metropolitan area.

- Abandoned vehicles litter the highway.
- Strange wildlife inhabits the overgrown streets.
- Small autonomous machines patrol intersections.
- An old distribution warehouse contains food and medical supplies.

The team discovers that the city wasn't abandoned in the traditional sense.

Its residents appear to have vanished.

**Discovery:** A newspaper dated 47 years in the future, featuring a photograph of a city that looks almost identical to Earth.

#### Area 2: The Commercial District

Deeper into the city, the expedition discovers an enormous shopping district.

- Department stores contain clothing, equipment, and consumer technology.
- Underground parking garages contain abandoned security robots.
- A police station contains weapons and ammunition.
- A functioning automated pharmacy is locked behind security doors.

The player discovers a collection of old emergency recordings.

They describe an event called **The Great Disappearance**.

According to the recordings, entire neighborhoods had once vanished from this world.

**Discovery:** Westwood Pines isn't the first place to arrive here.

#### Area 3: The Central Exchange

The expedition reaches a massive underground transportation hub.

At its center is an enormous machine that appears to be receiving energy from somewhere beneath the city.

Automated defense units protect the central chamber.

The player must:
- Restore power to the terminal.
- Access its central data archive.
- Recover the city's dimensional navigation records.
- Escape before the facility activates its defense network.

**Major discovery:** The city contains records of dimensional disturbances, and one entry describes a signal originating from somewhere called Earth.

The archive also identifies a location called the **Lazarus Research Complex**.

**Expedition reward**
- Medical and food supply unlocks.
- New crafting materials.
- City navigation coordinates.
- First major clue to the way home.

### Expedition 02: Project Lazarus

*Research and horror.*

**Story premise**

Rick recognizes the name Lazarus.

He becomes visibly uncomfortable.

He explains that before the experiment, he had discovered references to a mysterious research organization studying parallel dimensions. He claims he never worked for them.

The data recovered from the Dead City suggests that Lazarus built technology almost identical to his own.

The neighborhood council authorizes a second expedition to investigate.

**Objective:** Find the Lazarus facility, recover dimensional research, and determine whether its technology can reverse the displacement.

#### Area 1: The Research Perimeter

The facility is buried inside a dense forest.

- Abandoned research vehicles sit outside the perimeter.
- Security drones continue their patrol routes.
- Scientists' personal belongings are scattered around the entrance.
- Underground access tunnels lead into the complex.

The player finds evidence that the facility was evacuated in a hurry.

**Discovery:** A security recording shows scientists watching something emerge from a dimensional portal.

The recording ends with the facility going into lockdown.

#### Area 2: The Biological Wing

The expedition descends into the laboratory's biological research division.

Something has gone terribly wrong.

- Containment chambers have been broken open.
- Strange organisms have grown through the ventilation systems.
- Experimental creatures roam the corridors.
- Some machines appear to be protecting the organisms instead of attacking them.

The player must restore ventilation, access specimen storage, and retrieve biological research samples.

**Discovery:** Lazarus was studying organisms that could survive between dimensions.

One specimen is labeled:

**SUBJECT ZERO: ORIGIN UNKNOWN.**

Its biological structure appears to contain materials that don't exist in the known periodic table.

#### Area 3: The Dimensional Chamber

At the bottom of the complex is an enormous experimental chamber.

A portal device remains partially operational.

Its walls are covered in equations, coordinates, and warnings.

The player must activate the chamber, retrieve its core, and survive the facility's automated containment response.

**Major discovery:** Lazarus successfully transported objects between dimensions long before Rick's experiment.

But the research contains a terrifying revelation.

The process doesn't simply move objects.

It creates a connection between two realities.

And something on the other side can sometimes follow that connection.

Rick recognizes the final research entry.

It is signed by a scientist named Richard Halden.

He insists someone forged his name.

**Expedition reward**
- Advanced crafting technology.
- Biological samples.
- Portal component.
- Rick's first major secret exposed.

### Expedition 03: The Last Convoy

*Combat and high-value loot.*

**Story premise**

The Lazarus facility reveals that a military organization once attempted to transport a dimensional stabilizer across the Elsewhere.

The convoy disappeared before reaching its destination.

A nearby signal tower suddenly begins transmitting an emergency military frequency.

The message repeats:

> "Convoy command to anyone receiving. Cargo remains intact. Do not allow the machines to recover the package. Repeat. Do not allow them to recover the package."

Rick believes the cargo might contain the missing component needed to stabilize the neighborhood's return.

**Objective:** Locate the convoy, recover its cargo, and retrieve the dimensional stabilizer.

#### Area 1: The Crash Site

The convoy lies scattered across a massive open wasteland.

- Destroyed armored transports.
- Scavenger camps.
- Abandoned supply containers.
- Patrolling mechanical wildlife.

Players can search the wreckage for ammunition, weapons, fuel, and military equipment.

**Discovery:** The convoy was attacked from underground.

Something enormous dug its way through the earth.

#### Area 2: The Armored Depot

The surviving cargo is stored inside a reinforced underground military depot.

The entrance is protected by automated turrets and security systems.

Players must find access codes, restore backup power, and unlock the storage facility.

Inside are:
- Advanced firearms.
- Experimental armor.
- Military-grade robotics.
- Rare crafting components.
- Classified military records.

**Discovery:** The military wasn't transporting the stabilizer to a research facility.

It was transporting it to something called **The Anchor**.

#### Area 3: The Buried Transport

The convoy's final transport is buried deep underground.

It is surrounded by an enormous mechanical organism that has incorporated parts of the vehicle into its own body.

The player must fight or evade the machine, recover the stabilizer, and activate the emergency extraction system.

**Major discovery:** The stabilizer contains coordinates for a dimensional anchor located somewhere beneath an enormous body of water.

The military knew about the Anchor.

They were trying to prevent something from reaching it.

**Expedition reward**
- Dimensional stabilizer.
- Advanced military equipment.
- Heavy weapon blueprints.
- Coordinates to the Drowned District.

### Expedition 04: The Drowned District

*Underwater exploration and survival.*

**Story premise**

The stabilizer points to an enormous submerged city.

Rick believes the Anchor is located somewhere beneath its central infrastructure.

The neighborhood begins preparing its first underwater expedition.

The fire department converts an old maintenance garage into a diving equipment workshop.

A local mechanic develops improvised oxygen tanks.

The Techy helps construct underwater navigation equipment.

The entire expedition depends on technology the survivors have never used before.

**Objective:** Find the dimensional Anchor beneath the flooded city.

#### Area 1: The Flooded Suburbs

The expedition enters a partially flooded residential district.

- Houses are submerged up to their rooftops.
- Boats drift between buildings.
- Strange aquatic creatures occupy swimming pools.
- Old homes contain personal belongings and useful equipment.

Players must navigate flooded houses and recover equipment from submerged garages.

**Discovery:** One house contains a family photograph featuring a person who looks exactly like someone living back in Westwood Pines.

The photograph is dated 200 years ago.

#### Area 2: The Sunken Metro

The expedition descends into a submerged subway network.

- Broken trains block passageways.
- Oxygen becomes increasingly important.
- Underwater machines patrol the tunnels.
- Electrical systems occasionally reactivate.

The player discovers a functioning transportation terminal.

Its records reveal that the city once maintained connections to dozens of other dimensional locations.

**Discovery:** The Anchor is not a transportation device.

It is a machine that controls the boundaries between realities.

#### Area 3: The Abyssal Core

At the deepest point of the city is an enormous mechanical structure.

It is partially covered in coral-like growths and strange organic material.

The expedition discovers a massive dimensional energy source.

The player must:
- Restore the core's auxiliary power.
- Recover the Anchor's control module.
- Avoid the creatures guarding the structure.
- Reach extraction before the chamber floods completely.

**Major discovery:** The Anchor is still active.

And it is connected to an object orbiting high above the planet.

The expedition uncovers the designation:

**THE FALLING STAR.**

**Expedition reward**
- Anchor control module.
- Underwater equipment blueprints.
- Rare aquatic materials.
- Coordinates to the crash site.

### Expedition 05: The Falling Star

*Endgame. A world-changing event.*

**Story premise**

The neighborhood's radio tower detects an enormous object entering the atmosphere.

It crashes approximately 30 miles from Westwood Pines.

The impact creates a massive energy pulse that temporarily disables every electronic device in the neighborhood.

Rick recognizes the object.

He has seen its design before.

In the Lazarus research archives.

The object is an autonomous dimensional vessel called **THE OBSERVER**.

The neighborhood council authorizes the largest expedition ever organized.

This time, the mission isn't just about supplies.

It's about finding out what brought them here.

**Objective:** Enter the crashed vessel, discover its purpose, and recover the technology needed to activate the Anchor.

#### Area 1: The Impact Zone

The crash site is surrounded by a massive impact crater.

- Strange metallic debris is scattered across the landscape.
- Mechanical creatures are emerging from the wreckage.
- Energy storms periodically sweep across the area.
- Other scavengers have already established camps nearby.

Players must secure an entrance to the vessel.

**Discovery:** The ship's outer shell is made from the same unidentified material found on the baseball Rick teleported eleven years earlier.

#### Area 2: The Living Ship

Inside, the vessel appears to be alive.

Its corridors shift.

Its doors respond to human presence.

Its internal machines repair damage automatically.

The player discovers a central intelligence controlling the vessel.

It identifies Westwood Pines as:

**AN UNAUTHORIZED DIMENSIONAL ARRIVAL.**

The vessel has been monitoring the neighborhood since the moment it appeared.

**Discovery:** The Observer has been tracking dimensional anomalies for thousands of years.

It has records of countless worlds that have disappeared.

#### Area 3: The Observer's Heart

At the center of the vessel is an enormous intelligence core.

The player reaches the central chamber and activates its communication system.

The intelligence reveals the truth:

Westwood Pines was not simply teleported into another universe.

Rick's experiment created a dimensional rupture.

The rupture connected Earth to a network of parallel realities.

And the Observer was already waiting for someone to open that door.

**Major discovery:**

Rick didn't invent dimensional travel.

He accidentally activated something that had been dormant for millions of years.

The Observer offers the survivors a way home.

But there is a condition.

The dimensional rupture must be closed from the other side.

And someone must remain behind to operate the Anchor.

**Expedition reward**
- Observer technology.
- Advanced crafting systems.
- Access to endgame regions.
- The coordinates to Earth's dimensional signature.

## 6. The bigger mystery: What Rick isn't telling anyone

The five expeditions uncover the technology needed to get home, but they also uncover a much bigger mystery.

Rick has been lying.

After the final expedition, the player returns to Westwood Pines and discovers that Rick has disappeared from his laboratory.

His research notes reveal that he had actually made contact with the Observer before the experiment.

He received a message warning him not to activate the machine.

He activated it anyway.

But the worst discovery is buried in a final recording:

> "I didn't choose the coordinates. I didn't choose the destination. I just thought I was opening a door. Something on the other side opened it for me."

Rick didn't accidentally teleport the neighborhood.

Something used Rick to bring 1,000 people into this universe.

And now the player must decide whether to trust the scientist who brought them here.

## 7. The endgame: The Road Home

Once the player has recovered all five major components, the neighborhood can begin constructing a functioning dimensional gateway.

But the Anchor requires three additional resources that can only be found in increasingly dangerous endgame expeditions:

| Resource | Location | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Quantum Core | The Machine Graveyard | Powers the Anchor |
| Reality Shard | The Shattered Expanse | Stabilizes the dimensional coordinates |
| Observer Key | The Forbidden Citadel | Opens the passage to Earth |

These become the foundations of the endgame.

The player must venture into three new regions, each with its own unique enemy ecosystem, environmental hazards, bosses, and rare loot.

Eventually, the Anchor becomes operational.

But the player discovers three possible endings.

### Ending 1: Go Home

The player activates the gateway and returns Westwood Pines to Earth.

The neighborhood reappears exactly where it disappeared.

But the player notices something strange.

There are now 1,001 people in the neighborhood.

Someone came back with them.

### Ending 2: The New World

The player discovers that the Elsewhere is filled with valuable resources and technological possibilities.

Instead of returning home, they convince the neighborhood council to remain.

Westwood Pines becomes the foundation of a new civilization.

### Ending 3: The Door Was Never Closed

The player learns that the Observer was attempting to escape something much worse.

Activating the Anchor doesn't bring the neighborhood home.

It opens a doorway between every connected reality.

The final cutscene shows something enormous looking through the portal.

And then the screen goes black.

## 8. The actual game loop

The neighborhood acts as the persistent home base. Everything the player does outside should contribute to its survival and development.

The gameplay cycle:

1. **Westwood Pines.** Prepare equipment, craft supplies, accept missions, talk to residents.
2. **Choose an expedition.** Select a region, destination, and objective.
3. **Enter the Elsewhere.** Scavenge, fight machines, explore, complete objectives, encounter other players.
4. **Extract.** Escape with collected resources, equipment, research, and valuables.
5. **Upgrade the neighborhood.** Build facilities, unlock technologies, improve defenses, and progress the story.

## 9. The neighborhood should actually evolve

One of the strongest parts of this concept is that the player has a reason to care about the people they're bringing supplies home to.

The neighborhood should be a living settlement.

| Location | What it becomes |
|---|---|
| High School | Command center and expedition headquarters |
| Grocery Store | Food storage and trading post |
| Fire Station | Medical center and rescue operations |
| Rick's House | Research laboratory |
| Hardware Store | Crafting and equipment workshop |
| Gas Station | Fuel production and vehicle maintenance |
| Community Park | Farming and resource production |
| Police Station | Weapons storage and security |
| Shopping Plaza | Merchant district |
| Residential Houses | Player housing and survivor population |

As the game progresses, new residents can join, buildings can be upgraded, and new storylines can unlock.

Some residents might even become expedition companions.

Others might disappear during raids, develop rivalries, steal supplies, or start factions.

The player should feel that every successful expedition makes life better for the 1,000 people waiting back home.

## 10. The tone and identity of the game

Lean heavily into the contrast between ordinary suburban America and absolutely incomprehensible science fiction.

Think:

- A perfectly normal suburban mailbox sitting in front of a house surrounded by alien trees.
- A giant mechanical spider walking past a big-box store.
- A neighborhood dad using a modified leaf blower to defend the community.
- A middle-school science teacher becoming the settlement's leading robotics engineer.
- A military veteran organizing patrols from the high school gymnasium.
- Rick insisting that everything is under control while his laboratory explodes for the third time.

The story should have humor, mystery, tension, and genuinely emotional moments.

It shouldn't be another generic post-apocalyptic wasteland.

It should feel like a thousand ordinary people accidentally became the first settlers of an impossible universe.
